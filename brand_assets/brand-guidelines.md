# AreaCodes Brand Guidelines

---

## 1. Brand Mission & Core Values

### Mission Statement

To empower local, independent businesses by helping them attract loyal customers without compromising their identity or profit.  

To reshape the way people engage with their city, making supporting local not just a nice thing to do, but the best choice.

### Core Values

- **Local over corporate**: We champion independent, grassroots enterprise.
- **Culture over convenience**: We believe in the unique, authentic character of a city.
- **Truth over polish**: We value authenticity and real-world experiences.
- **Community over competition**: We foster a network of support among local businesses.
- **Accessibility without devaluing**: We make local a viable and attractive option for everyone.

---

## 2. Tone of Voice & Messaging Principles

### Brand Personality

Urban and bold. Confident and not trying too hard. Supportive of independent businesses and local culture.  

The voice is raw, direct, and trustworthy, built around real people and real places. Not playful or luxurious — just grounded and accessible.

### Messaging Principles

- **No-bullshit**: Speak plainly and with clarity, avoiding jargon and corporate fluff.
- **Real, but warm**: Honest and direct, but with a supportive, community-oriented spirit.
- **Clear, punchy, minimal**: Messages are concise and impactful.
- **Insider's Guide**: Speak with the authority of someone in the know, helping users feel like they are part of the city's culture.

---

## 3. Logo Usage

### Logo Mark

The AreaCodes logo is a strong, modular wordmark designed to be highly adaptable.  
It functions like a stamp, sticker, or tag.

### Primary Logo

The primary logo is the full wordmark, **"AreaCodes."**  
It should be used in most applications where space allows.

### Lockups

- **City-Specific Lockup**: For future expansion, the logo can be combined with a city name, e.g., `AreaCodes: Brighton`.  
  This lockup reinforces the hyper-local model.

### Clear Space & Sizing

- **Clear Space**: Always maintain a minimum clear space around the logo.
- **Minimum Size**: The logo should not be reproduced at a size where the text becomes unreadable.

---

## 4. Color Palette

### Primary Palette

Black and white for typography and foundational design elements, providing a clean, high-contrast canvas.

### Accent Palette

The accent color is provided by rich, unfiltered photography of Brighton (or the relevant local area).  

This allows for natural, dynamic color without the need for a fixed secondary palette. The accent color changes with the imagery, reflecting the diversity and vibrancy of the local scene.

---

## 5. Typography Hierarchy

### Primary Font: Poppins Bold

Modern, clean, and highly readable.  

Its bold weight is used for all headlines, titles, and key messaging to create a confident and punchy visual voice.

---

## 6. Imagery & Photography Guidelines

Our visual identity relies heavily on a specific style of imagery.

### Photography Principles

- **Authenticity is Key**: All images must be real, vibrant, and captured locally.
- **Show the Real City**: Capture the grit, textures, and imperfections of Brighton's environment.
- **Focus on People & Places**: Feature real people and the unique, character-filled venues and shops that make the local scene special.

### Visual Direction

- Use textures like Google Maps-style overlays, gridlines, and street signs to reinforce the urban, geographic feel of the brand.
- Lean into Swiss design, minimalism, and industrial design for a strong visual direction — something that feels like a poster or flyer found in the street.

---

## 7. Do's and Don'ts

### DO'S

- Do use real, vibrant, and unfiltered photography.
- Do maintain a high-contrast black and white base.
- Do keep messaging punchy, direct, and honest.
- Do use Poppins Bold for all headlines.

### DON'TS

- Don't use stock photos or overly polished imagery.
- Don't use any colors outside of the black/white/photo-accent palette.
- Don't use a playful or whimsical tone.
- Don't resize the logo to the point of illegibility.
