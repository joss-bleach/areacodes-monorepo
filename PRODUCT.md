# Product

## Register

split: brand (apps/marketing) / product (apps/business, apps/admin, apps/map)

## Users

Two distinct user groups:

**Consumers** - city residents and urban explorers, 20s-40s, who want to support independent local businesses but need a compelling reason to choose them over corporate chains. Using the map app on mobile, often on-the-go, discovering places in their area. They care about authenticity and neighbourhood character, not just discounts.

**Business owners** - independent local businesses (restaurants, cafés, boutiques, markets, studios). Typically non-technical owners or managers. Using the business dashboard to create voucher offers, manage their profile, and track redemptions. They need a tool that respects their time and doesn't feel corporate or impersonal.

## Product Purpose

Areacodes connects independent local businesses with local customers through exclusive vouchers - making choosing local not just the ethical choice, but the smart one. The platform displaces corporate discount aggregators (Groupon et al.) and big-chain loyalty programmes by keeping all value within the local economy, no middlemen.

Success looks like: independent businesses growing their customer base, consumers discovering and returning to local spots, and a city that feels alive with real character rather than copy-paste high streets.

## Brand Personality

Bold, direct, community-first.

Three words: **Honest. Sharp. Local.**

The brand doesn't perform authenticity - it IS authentic. No corporate fluff, no aspirational stock-photo lifestyle. Real places, real people, real city. Tone is confident, warm but never sentimental, and always on the side of the independent over the corporate.

Emotions to evoke for consumers: civic pride, discovery, solidarity. For businesses: confidence, belonging, ease.

## Anti-references

- **AI SaaS cream/sand aesthetic** - warm-tinted near-white body backgrounds, soft geometric sans, card grid layouts. The 2024-2026 AI default. This project must feel hand-crafted and human, not AI-generated.
- **Big-tech / FAANG product UIs** - Material Design, Fluent, corporate gloss. Anything that looks like it could be a Google or Microsoft product.
- **Anything that screams AI or unauthentic** - stock photography, generic iconography, soft gradients, glassy surfaces, hero metrics with big numbers. The brand should feel like it was designed by someone who actually cares about independent culture, not assembled from a SaaS template.
- **Groupon / discount aggregator UIs** - cheapening visual language that makes offers feel like bargain-bin coupons rather than genuine local perks.

## Design Principles

1. **Truth over polish** - Real photography, direct language, no softening edges to make things more palatable. The brand's honesty is its credibility.
2. **Sharp or nothing** - Zero radius, tight tracking, bold weight. Decisiveness in every design choice. Ambiguity is not an option.
3. **Community, not corporate** - Every interface choice should feel like it was made by someone who shops local, not by a product team trying to appear relatable.
4. **Contrast as communication** - The black/white foundation is not a limitation; it's the system. Color, space, and typography do the work. When color appears, it means something.
5. **Never perform authenticity** - Avoid the visual clichés of "authentic" branding (kraft paper textures, hand-drawn elements, rustic fonts). Authentic means honest and direct, not aesthetically rustic.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. All text and interactive elements must meet minimum contrast ratios (4.5:1 body, 3:1 large text). The true black/white palette makes this achievable and non-negotiable - never soften colors to the point of failing contrast.

Reduced motion support required on all animations. All interactive elements must be keyboard-navigable. Screen reader compatibility expected across marketing and business app surfaces.
